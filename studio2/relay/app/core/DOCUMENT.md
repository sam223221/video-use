## tls.py — the SAN constraint filter (the load-bearing v2 logic)

- `_PERMITTED_NETS` is the **candidate pool** and INCLUDES `100.64.0.0/10`
  (CGNAT/Tailscale) — unlike v1, where it was the hard filter.
- Candidates (localhost, `<hostname>.local`, 127.0.0.1, private/CGNAT IPv4s)
  are then filtered against the **CA's own parsed nameConstraints**
  (per-RFC 5280 per-type semantics: a type with no permitted entries is
  unrestricted; excluded subtrees always apply). Under the production v1 CA
  (`.local`/`localhost` + RFC1918/loopback) CGNAT SANs are dropped — a SAN
  the CA can't vouch for would break the WHOLE chain on constraint-enforcing
  platforms. A future widened CA grows the SANs automatically (logged at
  INFO when candidates are dropped).
- Leaf regen reasons: v1's set, with the lan_ip/hostname drift checks
  **generalized** to "the desired (filtered) SAN set must be covered by the
  SANs on disk" — detects DHCP drift AND grows SANs for newly permitted
  addresses, and cannot regen-loop on an address the CA refuses (deviation
  from v1 documented in the module docstring).
- A CA whose constraints permit NO usable SAN → loud error + HTTP-only
  (a SAN-less leaf would be unusable).
- `get_state()` (mtime-cached, never raises), `ca_der()` (the SHARED CA →
  byte-identical to v1's `/ca.crt`), `set_serving()`/`is_enabled()` carried
  from v1. `ca_regenerated_this_boot()` is a constant `False` — v2 never
  regenerates the CA.
